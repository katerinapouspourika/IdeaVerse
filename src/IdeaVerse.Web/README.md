# IdeaVerse.Web

The IdeaVerse web app: plan ideas with a target date, list the components each needs, and share them with your company or team in a workspace.

## Getting Started

Start the database and the API, then the web dev server:

```bash
docker compose up -d db mail
dotnet run --project src/IdeaVerse.Api -- --environment Development --urls http://localhost:5080
cd src/IdeaVerse.Web
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open `http://localhost:5173`. The dev server forwards `/api` to the API, so the app and the API share an origin and the login cookie works as in production. Set `IDEAVERSE_API_URL` to point the proxy at a different API address.

## Features

- **Public pages** — signed-out visitors land on the home page at `/`, with `/how-it-works` (recorded walkthrough videos of the app), `/contact` (a form the API emails to the team), `/pricing` (free during early access, with answers to common questions in `src/marketing/faq.ts`, also given to search engines as FAQ structured data), `/privacy` and `/terms`, and `/templates`, a gallery of ready-made ideas with a page each at `/templates/<slug>` (what it includes, who it is best for, and a preview of the idea it creates, with "Use this template" above and below the list). Templates live in `src/templates/templates.ts`: each fills in a new idea's title, description, tags, and date, and adds its checklist once saved (a title with a part to fill in, such as "Launch: [product name]", is offered as the title's placeholder and hint instead, so its brackets are never saved), whether started from its page ("Use this template") or picked under "Start from" in the New idea form. A visitor who picks one before signing up is told it is waiting, and it opens in the New idea form once they have signed in and have a workspace (remembered in the browser by `src/templates/pendingTemplate.ts`). `/use-cases` and `/compare` (with a page each under them) show how different teams use IdeaVerse and how it compares with spreadsheets, to-do apps, and project-management suites; their content is in `src/solutions/content.ts`, compares against ways of working rather than named products, and says where the alternative is the better choice. The template, use case, and comparison lists end with the home page's sign-up band (`src/components/CtaBand.tsx`). The Privacy Policy and Terms (`src/legal/legal.ts`) are drafts that describe what the app does, with details only the business can supply in [brackets]; they say they await legal review and are kept out of search engines (`index: false` in `src/seo/pages.ts`) until then. The header groups Templates, Use cases, and Compare under Explore, and turns into a menu button on narrow screens, where they are listed under an Explore heading.
- **Accounts** — sign up, sign in, and sign out with the API's cookie login. Signed-out visitors opening a signed-in page are sent to the login page and returned to where they were going. A new password needs at least 12 characters, with no other rules; the forms tick the rule off as it is met (`src/auth/passwordRules.ts`), and every password field has a button to show or hide what was typed.
- **Dashboard** — signed in, `/` greets the user, offers a getting-started checklist until its steps are done or it is hidden (its idea steps count only the user's own ideas, and creating the workspace and inviting a teammate are listed only for the workspace's owner), and shows the current workspace at a glance: counts of active (not done), overdue, due-this-week, and done ideas, the ideas that need attention, the components assigned to them, and their latest notifications (reminders, assignments, comments, and mentions). A workspace with no ideas yet shows "Add your first idea" instead of the counts and lists, with a blank idea and three starter templates (`src/templates/StarterTemplates.tsx`).
  - **Settings** — the name (or email) in the header opens `/settings`, where the user sets the name others see, changes their email (confirmed through a link to the new address) and password, deletes their account, picks their time zone (a search box filters the list, each zone shows its current UTC offset, old spellings such as `Asia/Calcutta` appear under their current names, and the IANA id is what is saved; `src/api/timeZones.ts`) and which reminders they get (starting soon, coming up, due tomorrow, due today, overdue), whether they are also emailed, and whether comments on their ideas are emailed too (off by default; mentions are always emailed). A menu of its sections (a sticky side menu on wide screens, jump links above the cards on phones) links to each card by anchor (`#profile`, `#email`, `#password`, `#time-zone`, `#reminders`, `#workspace`, `#delete-account`). The time zone and the reminder and email switches save as soon as they change (a time zone search saves its first match once typing pauses), with a small "Saved" beside the card's heading, and go back to the last saved value with the reason if a save fails; the name, email, and password forms keep their own buttons. An account without a time zone gets the browser's on first sign-in. Owners and admins of the current workspace also find its settings there, under Workspace: its logo and colour.
  - New accounts confirm their email first: sign-up shows "Check your inbox", the emailed link opens `/confirm-email`, which confirms the address, signs the new account in, and opens its ideas; signing in before confirming explains why and offers to resend the link.
  - "Forgot your password?" on the sign-in page emails a link to `/reset-password`, where the user chooses a new one. Locally, these emails arrive in Mailpit at `http://localhost:8025`.
- **Workspaces** — the header's workspace menu (a button showing the current workspace) lists the user's workspaces, each with its badge and their role, the current one checked, to switch between them (the choice is remembered in the browser), and, set apart at the end, New workspace, which starts one at `/workspaces/new`. Someone in no workspace yet sees a welcome page: with invitations, those come first, with a form to start their own below; otherwise a two-step setup, naming the workspace (with an optional "Your name", saved as the display name so invited teammates see it rather than the email address) and then inviting the team at `/workspaces/<id>/invite` (several addresses at once, as members or admins, or "Skip for now"), which leads to Home and its Get started checklist, or to the ideas page when a template chosen before signing up is waiting. Every new workspace, from the welcome page or `/workspaces/new`, goes through the invite step. People (for owners and admins) also has the workspace's invite link: turn it on, copy it, replace it, or turn it off. Opening a link at `/join/<token>` shows the workspace and joins it as a member; someone signed out is asked to sign in or sign up first, and the link waits for them in the browser (`src/workspaces/pendingJoin.ts`). People also lets owners and admins set the company domain (their own work email's), so colleagues signed in at that domain see the workspace under "Your team may already be here" on the welcome and new-workspace pages and can ask to join; requests appear at the top of People under "Join requests" with Approve and Decline (the header's People link counts them, and the request email opens People on that workspace with `?workspace=<id>`), and the workspace can be hidden from the company again. In Settings, under **Workspace** (which the People page links to as "Logo and colour"), the current workspace's owners and admins pick its colour from a preset palette (`src/workspaces/workspaceColors.ts`, each readable with white text) and upload a logo; the header's workspace menu shows the logo, or the workspace's initial on its colour. Fonts stay the app's own.
- **People** — `/people` lists the current workspace's people and roles. Owners and admins invite people by email, one or many at once: a pasted or typed list (split at commas, semicolons, spaces, and new lines; `Name <address>` entries give the address) becomes removable chips, addresses that are not valid, already in the workspace, or already invited are marked with the reason and not sent, repeats are left out with a note, and the rest are invited with one role, each on its own, so a refused address stays with the API's reason while the others go (the same sending as the invite step, `src/workspaces/sendInvitations.ts`). They also change other people's roles (after confirming, with a message once saved; nobody changes their own), remove people, resend or revoke open invitations (after confirming), and rename the workspace; anyone but the owner can leave. The owner's danger zone hands the workspace to someone else, or deletes it after its name is typed.
- **Invitations** — the header shows "Invitations (n)" while any are open. `/invitations`, where invitation emails link, lists the user's open invitations to join or decline (declining asks first). Joining opens the workspace.
- **Ideas list** — `/ideas`: the current workspace's ideas (adding one confirms it in a toast naming it, with Open to go to its page) with status filters, a search box, a tag filter, and a choice of order (target date, title, recently changed, newest, most votes, impact for effort), plus overdue and due-this-week summaries, component progress, tags, and whose idea it is. The Archived tab shows archived ideas. A workspace with no ideas yet (none archived either) shows no filters, order, or views, only a way to add a blank idea or start from one of three templates (`src/templates/StarterTemplates.tsx`). The list remembers its view, status tab, tag, and order per workspace in the browser, so a reload opens it as it was left; the search box starts empty. On a phone (600px wide or less, `useNarrowScreen`) the list shows only the search box and a Filters button, which counts the active status and tag filters and opens a panel with the status tabs, tag, and order (Escape or Close filters closes it); the views are icon buttons named for screen readers. Every tag chip, on the list, the board, the side panel, or an idea's page, links to `/ideas?tag=<tag>`, which sets the tag filter and drops the parameter from the address; list cards open their idea through a title link stretched over the card, so the tags inside stay links of their own. The Board view has a column per status: drag an idea to another column, or use its status menu from the keyboard, to change its status (with Undo); ideas the user cannot edit stay put. The Calendar view lays the ideas out on a month grid by target date; an idea with a start date also shows on its start day as an outlined chip (named "Starts: …" for screen readers), with a thin line on each day in between (up to three a day, then a count of the rest). The Timeline view shows each idea as a bar from its start date to its target date, coloured by status and red when overdue, against a scale of months with a line for today; an idea with no start date is a dot on its target date. It scrolls sideways for long roadmaps, and each row also gives its status, start (or "no start date"), and target date in words. On a phone (600px wide or less, `useNarrowScreen`), where grid cells and bars are too small to read, the Calendar lists the month's ideas grouped by target date, each with its status and start date, and the Timeline lists the ideas grouped by the month they are due, each with its start (or "no start date") and target date in words; their links behave as on the grid and the timeline. The Matrix view places scored ideas on a 2×2 of impact against effort (quick wins, big bets, nice to haves, time sinks; a score of 4–5 counts as high and 1–2 as low), lists ideas with a medium 3 on either axis as Balanced (on the centre line, with their scores), and lists the ones not scored yet. Each idea card has a vote button showing its vote count. Clicking an idea in the list, the board (anywhere on its card), the timeline (anywhere on its row, bar included), or the matrix opens it in a side panel (`?idea=<id>` in the address, so it can be shared and Back closes it) with its details, priority, and checklist, and a link to the full page; a click with Ctrl, ⌘, or Shift, or the middle button, opens the page as a link does.
- **Notifications** — a bell in the header shows how many notifications are unread, checked every minute, and previews the latest six in a drop-down (each with a short title, its idea, how long ago it arrived and a dot while unread, under Mark all read and above a link to see them all); where the browser supports it, the installed app's icon shows the same count. The notifications page lists them newest first, opens the idea (marking the reminder read, and saying so when that switches the workspace), and marks them all read; opening a comment, mention, or assignment scrolls to that comment or component (`/ideas/{id}#comment-{commentId}` or `#component-{componentId}`), moves focus to it and briefly highlights it; for people in more than one workspace, the page and the drop-down name each notification's workspace. Reminder emails go to Mailpit locally, at `http://localhost:8025`.
- **AI help** — its panels show the workspace's remaining requests for the day. When the API doesn't have it set up, its buttons stay visible but greyed out (`aria-disabled`, still focusable), described by a note that AI help isn’t set up for the workspace yet (`UnavailableAiButton` in `ai/AiAllowance.tsx`; beside the button on an idea, in a bubble on hover, focus, or tap on the ideas list). "Brainstorm with AI" on the ideas list turns a goal into scored ideas, each of which opens the new idea form filled in. On an idea its team can edit, "Suggest with AI" proposes components to tick and add, and "Improve with AI" shows a critique and a sharper version to use or dismiss.
- **Idea page** — the work (details, components, discussion, activity) on the left and its schedule, priority, and team on the right on wide screens; on narrow screens they stack in reading order: the details, the components and discussion, then the schedule, priority, and team, and the activity last; a "⋯" menu holds archive, restore, and delete. An archived idea is read-only apart from its discussion and votes (no editing, status, checklist, scores, postponing, or team changes; `canChange` in `api/types.ts`), under a banner whose Restore button, for its owner and the workspace's admins, brings it back. Under the title, one row of chips sums the idea up: its status, target date (red, and saying "Overdue", once it has passed), planned start, owner, and tags; the team can change the status straight from its chip, a menu (with Undo). "Edit details" beside the title edits the details, status, and tags (typed tags become chips on Enter or a comma, removed with their × or Backspace), plan the start from the Schedule card ("Plan the start": a date and an optional time, shown among the chips under the title, which the team is reminded of ahead of time; it can also be set in the new idea form), postpone from the Schedule card's Postpone menu ("+1 week" or "+1 month" from the target date, offered only when that isn't in the past, or "Pick a date…" for a later date; with Undo, which restores the date and status and doesn’t count the postponement), archive or restore (owner and workspace admins), check off components (rename one in place by clicking its title, where Enter saves and Escape cancels; reorder them by dragging or with the arrow keys on their handle; and remove one, with Undo, from its "⋯" menu), and manage the team, which is chosen from the workspace's people. People outside the idea's team see it read-only; deleting and managing the team are for the idea's owner and the workspace's admins, and team members can leave instead. Opening an idea from another workspace switches the header to that workspace. Components can be assigned to someone in the workspace with a due date, shown as chips on the component; clicking a chip, or "Assignee and due date" in its "⋯" menu, opens one small form for the assignee, due date, and notes, and "Assign to me" (in the menu or the form) takes it on. The due date chip says, in words and colour, when it is overdue (red) or due within the next two days (amber), by the user's own calendar. Anyone in the workspace can vote for the idea with the thumbs-up beside its title (also in the side panel). The Priority card lets its team score its impact and effort from 1 to 5, saved as soon as they are picked, which places it in a matrix quadrant, or Balanced when either score is 3. Below the team, everyone in the workspace can discuss the idea in comments (editing their own; admins can delete any), typing `@` to mention someone in the workspace, who is notified (the `mentions/` folder turns `@Name` into the API's mention tokens and back), and an Activity section shows who changed what, loaded when opened, ten entries at a time with Previous and Next.
- **Search and shortcuts** — the header's search button, Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac), or / opens a command palette that finds the current workspace's ideas by title or description (a whole-title match first, then titles starting with the text, then a word starting with it, then the rest) and runs common actions (new idea, go to a page, switch workspace) from the keyboard. Before anything is typed it lists up to five ideas recently opened in the workspace, remembered in the browser (`src/commands/recentIdeas.ts`). Tab on a highlighted idea lists what can be done with it: open it, open it in the ideas page's side panel, copy its link, and, for those who may change it, set its status (with Undo); Shift+Tab or Escape goes back to the search. Outside text fields, N starts a new idea, G then H, I, P, N, or S goes to Home, Ideas, People, Notifications, or Settings, and ? lists the shortcuts. Shortcuts wait while another dialog is open.
- **Install and offline** — IdeaVerse is an installable web app: `public/manifest.webmanifest` gives its name, icons (`public/icons/`, made from `branding/ideaverse-logo.png`, with a maskable one for Android), shortcuts, and the screenshots browsers show in their install dialog (`public/screenshots/`). The browser's bar above the installed app matches the header in each colour scheme: `index.html` has a `theme-color` tag per scheme, and `src/theme.ts` points both at the scheme picked in the header. Where the browser offers it, the account menu has Install app. `public/sw.js`, registered in production builds only (`src/registerServiceWorker.ts`), never stores API calls, serves the content-named files in `/assets/` from its cache, and tries the network first for pages, falling back to the last copy seen or a short offline page; bump its `VERSION` to drop what earlier versions cached. When the API can't be reached, signed-in pages say so and offer Try again, rather than sending people to sign in.

## Usage Examples

In production the API serves the built app from its `wwwroot`, on the same origin. The Docker image does this when built with `WEB_PROJECT=IdeaVerse.Web`, as `compose.yaml` does:

```bash
docker compose up --build
```

### Recording the walkthroughs

`scripts/record-walkthroughs.mjs` signs up two demo accounts, confirms them through the emails Mailpit caught, and records one short video per walkthrough (1024×640, saved as H.264 MP4 and as VP9 WebM for browsers without H.264), starting once the app is on screen, with a WebP poster image, for each walkthrough listed in `src/marketing/walkthroughs.ts`. The walkthroughs build on each other, so they are always recorded together. The How it works page shows each poster with a play button in the middle until the video is played. Re-record after visible changes to the app:

```bash
docker compose up --build -d
BASE_URL=http://localhost:8080 npm run record:walkthroughs
```

It needs an ffmpeg with libx264, libvpx-vp9 and libwebp (set `FFMPEG` when it is not on the PATH). Set `CHROMIUM_PATH` when Playwright has no browser of its own installed, and `MAILPIT_URL` when Mailpit is not at `http://localhost:8025`. Videos keep their file names, and the API lets browsers cache them for a day, so returning visitors may see the previous recording for up to a day. After recording, update `walkthroughsRecordedOn` in `src/marketing/walkthroughs.ts`, which the videos' structured data reports.

### Interface building blocks

- `components/Feedback.tsx`: `useConfirm()` opens a styled confirmation dialog (focus trapped, Escape cancels) and resolves to the answer; `useToast()` shows a short message at the bottom (at the top on a phone), optionally with Undo or another action (such as Open after adding an idea). Two show at most; plain ones go after 5 seconds or when the user moves to another page, ones with a button after 8 seconds, and any can be dismissed. Use them instead of `window.confirm` and inline success text; reversible actions (archive, removing a component, changing the status, postponing) offer Undo rather than asking first; undoing a postponement takes it back through the API so it isn't counted.
- `components/Menu.tsx`: a "⋯"-style menu of actions, keyboard friendly (arrow keys, Home, End, Escape); items can carry a second line and a dividing line above; used for the idea's less frequent actions, the account and theme menus, and the workspace menu.
- `components/Skeleton.tsx`: loading placeholders in the shape of the content.
- `components/ErrorMessage.tsx`: `ErrorMessage` shows why a form submission failed; `LoadError` shows that something on the page couldn't load, in plain words, with Try again to load it again (a refusal the API explains, such as a 403, shows its own message instead). Use `LoadError` for query failures.
- Icons come from `lucide-react`; mark decorative ones `aria-hidden`.
- The header's theme menu picks light, dark, or the device's colour scheme (`src/theme.ts`); its icon shows the scheme in effect. The choice is remembered in the browser and applied by `index.html` before the page paints.
- The layout has a "Skip to content" link, moves focus to the page after each navigation, and honours "reduce motion". Public pages (those whose route `handle` is `fullWidth`) end with the full footer of Product, Explore, and Company links; signed-in app pages get a slim one-line footer with Help (the How it works page), Privacy, and Terms, except on phones, where the bottom bar takes its place.
- The header fits phones down to 320px wide without scrolling sideways: below 600px it keeps the page's 16px gutter, and signed in, below 420px it shows the logo without the IdeaVerse name (the link keeps it as its accessible name).
- On phones (700px wide and narrower, `phoneQuery` in `src/components/useMediaQuery.ts`, the width at which toasts move to the top), the signed-in app's pages in a workspace have a bottom bar with Home, Ideas, New (which opens the New idea form), Notifications (with the unread count), and People (with the requests to join), the current page marked; it sits above the home indicator's safe area, the page leaves room for it, and it steps aside while a text field has focus. The header then shows the current workspace's badge and name beside the logo instead of the IdeaVerse name and the bell, and its menu keeps only the workspace switcher and invitations.

### Search engines and link previews

- Every page sets its browser title, description, and `robots` tag with `usePageMeta` (`src/seo/`). Sign-in, account, and signed-in pages are `noindex`. The hook also keeps the prerendered canonical URL in step with client-side navigation.
- The public pages are listed in `src/seo/pages.ts`. After `vite build`, `scripts/prerender.mjs` renders each of them, and a "not found" page, to static HTML through `src/prerender.tsx` (a `vite build --ssr` entry), as a signed-out visitor sees them. Search engines, AI crawlers, and link previews get real content, a canonical URL, Open Graph and Twitter tags with `public/og-image.png` (1200×630), and structured data: Organization and WebSite on the home page, a VideoObject per walkthrough on How it works.
- The build writes `index.html`, `how-it-works.html`, `contact.html`, `pricing.html`, `privacy.html`, `terms.html`, `templates.html`, `use-cases.html`, and `compare.html`, a page per item under `templates/`, `use-cases/`, and `compare/`, `404.html`, the empty app shell `app.html`, `sitemap.xml`, and `robots.txt`; the API serves each for its routes. Absolute URLs use `SITE_URL`, which production builds must set (`compose.yaml` passes `http://localhost:8080` to the Dockerfile's `SITE_URL` build argument). Without it the build leaves out canonical URLs, preview images, structured data, and the sitemap, rather than point them somewhere wrong. `app.html` is always `noindex`.
- In the browser, the app renders over the prerendered HTML. Returning users skip the public home page while their dashboard loads, and see a plain header (logo and theme button) rather than the public links until their account loads: `src/auth/sessionHint.ts` remembers a signed-in visit in `localStorage`, and a script in `index.html` hides the prerendered home page for them, revealing it after three seconds should the app fail to start.
- Signed-in pages are loaded on demand (`src/routes.tsx`), so visitors to the public pages download only what those pages need.
- To add a public page: add it to `publicPages` in `src/seo/pages.ts` and to `routes.tsx`, and add its route and file to `PublicPages` in the API's `WebAppExtensions.cs`. A new signed-in page goes in `routes.tsx` and the API's `AppRoutes`.

## Recommendations

> [!NOTE]
> The API types in `src/api/types.ts` are written by hand to mirror `src/IdeaVerse.Api`. Update them in the same change as the API contract.

> [!NOTE]
> "Today" follows the account's time zone (the browser's until the account has one), matching the API's notion of "today" and "overdue". When the browser's time zone differs from the account's, as while travelling, a prompt above each signed-in page (`components/TimeZonePrompt`) offers to switch to it or keep the saved one; keeping it is remembered in this browser for that time zone, so the prompt returns only from somewhere else.

> [!NOTE]
> The look is "bright and friendly": colours, radii, and shadows are CSS variables at the top of `src/styles.css`, built from the logo's navy and sky blue with a warm yellow accent, and redefined for dark mode. In light mode the page is a warm linen (`--bg`) with white cards (`--surface`), so each card stands apart from the page. The font is Nunito, bundled from `@fontsource-variable/nunito` rather than loaded from Google.

> [!NOTE]
> The logo in `public/` (`logo.png`, `favicon.ico`, `apple-touch-icon.png`) is cut from the original at `branding/ideaverse-logo.png`, with its white background made transparent. Regenerate all three from that file when the logo changes. In dark mode the logo sits on a white tile, because the navy lines do not show on a dark background.
